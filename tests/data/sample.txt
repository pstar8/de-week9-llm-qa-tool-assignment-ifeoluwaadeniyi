The Physiocrats rejected this entire framework. They argued that wealth consisted not of gold and silver, but of real goods—particularly agricultural products—that sustained life and provided the material basis for all economic activity. They contended that mercantilist policies, by diverting resources toward manufacturing and commerce and by imposing artificial restrictions on agriculture, had actually impoverished France by suppressing the only truly productive sector of the economy. Where mercantilists saw state intervention as essential, Physiocrats saw it as the primary obstacle to prosperity. Where mercantilists focused on trade and manufacturing, Physiocrats insisted on the primacy of agriculture.
